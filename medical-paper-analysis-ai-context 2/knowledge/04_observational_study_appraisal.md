# Observational Study Appraisal

## Purpose

Appraise cohort, case-control, and cross-sectional studies.

## When To Use

Use when exposures or treatments were not assigned by investigators. Also apply its confounding checks alongside `03_clinical_trial_appraisal.md` when an assigned intervention is compared nonrandomly or with a historical control.

## Core Checklist

- Design: cohort, case-control, or cross-sectional.
- Population and selection: source population, inclusion/exclusion, recruitment, loss to follow-up.
- Temporality: did exposure clearly precede outcome?
- Exposure measurement: accuracy, timing, misclassification.
- Outcome measurement: validity, blinding, consistent ascertainment.
- Confounding: known confounders, confounding by indication, residual confounding.
- Adjustment strategy: model variables, propensity methods, matching, stratification.
- Bias: selection bias, information bias, immortal time bias, recall bias.
- Missing data: amount, mechanism, imputation or sensitivity analyses.
- Effect size: adjusted and unadjusted estimates, confidence intervals.
- Sensitivity analyses: alternative definitions, negative controls, unmeasured confounding checks.
- External validity: who results apply to.

STROBE is a reporting guideline, not by itself a quality score. Reported well does not mean methodologically strong.

## Common Pitfalls

- Inferring causation from association.
- Ignoring temporality in cross-sectional studies.
- Treating adjusted models as fully eliminating confounding.
- Missing measurement differences between groups.

## Minimal Example

Synthetic finding: "High biomarker levels were associated with disease." Ask whether biomarker was measured before disease, after disease, or because of disease.
