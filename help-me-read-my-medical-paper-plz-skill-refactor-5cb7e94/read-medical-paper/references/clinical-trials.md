# Clinical Trial Appraisal

## Purpose

Appraise randomized, nonrandomized, and single-arm interventional clinical trials using design-appropriate reporting and critical-appraisal prompts.

## When To Use

Use whenever investigators assign a clinical intervention, including early-phase, single-arm, nonrandomized, superiority, noninferiority, and equivalence trials.

## Core Checklist

- Trial question: population, intervention, comparator, outcomes, timing.
- Design and phase: dose escalation/expansion, single-arm, randomized, superiority, noninferiority, or equivalence.
- Comparator: concurrent randomized, concurrent nonrandomized, historical, or absent; is that choice adequate for the claim?
- Assignment: randomization method, balance, stratification, or likely allocation bias when nonrandomized.
- Allocation concealment when randomized: could assignment be predicted?
- Blinding: participants, clinicians, outcome assessors, analysts.
- Eligibility: inclusion/exclusion and who was left out.
- Endpoints: primary, secondary, exploratory; patient-centered or surrogate.
- Sample size and power: planned assumptions and achieved enrollment.
- Analysis set: intention-to-treat, per-protocol, safety population.
- Missing data: amount, reasons, handling, sensitivity analyses.
- Effect size: absolute and relative effects, confidence intervals, clinical importance.
- Harms: adverse events, serious adverse events, discontinuations.
- Subgroups: prespecified or post hoc; interaction tests.
- Follow-up: duration and attrition.
- Protocol/statistical-analysis-plan deviations, early stopping, and prespecified dose-limiting-toxicity or stopping rules when applicable.
- Conflicts of interest and sponsor role.

For noninferiority/equivalence:

- Is the margin justified clinically?
- Are both intention-to-treat and per-protocol analyses considered?
- Is "not statistically different" incorrectly treated as equivalent?

## Common Pitfalls

- Confusing statistical significance with clinical importance.
- Ignoring harms when benefits are modest.
- Overreading secondary endpoints after a negative primary endpoint.
- Accepting a noninferiority conclusion without checking the margin.

## Minimal Example

Synthetic result: "Risk ratio 0.80, 95% CI 0.60 to 1.05." The interval includes no clear benefit and may include clinically different effects; interpret with endpoint importance and trial power.
